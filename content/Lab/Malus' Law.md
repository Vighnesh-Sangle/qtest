## Introduction
Usually, electromagnetic waves have their electric and magnetic field vectors oscillating in all directions. But using a polaroid, we can only let waves oscillating along a particular axis to pass.

Further, given already polarized light, it is passed through another polaroid which is at an angle with respect to the light, Malus' Law tells us that the resulting intensity is directly proportional to the cosine squared of the angle. In this experiment, we verify Malus's Law
## Experiment Setup and Procedure

#### Apparatus
Laser Source, Polaroid, intensity measuring device, stands for them all.

#### Procedure
The second polaroid is slowly turned and the angle for which the intensity measured is maximum is noted.

Then the polaroid is turned $10\degree$ each time and intensity is noted till the total angle turned is $90\degree$ and intensity is zero.
Then the graph of $cos^2\theta$ and Intensity($I$) is measured.
## Formulae and Relations
If $\theta$ is the difference between original the polarization of the light and the polaroid's angle, $I_0$ is the original intensity, and intensity of the light after passing through the polaroid is:$$ I = I_0 \ cos^2\theta$$
